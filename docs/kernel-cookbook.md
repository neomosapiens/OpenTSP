# Kernel cookbook

The idioms a kernel writer needs and the specification does not spell out: how to divide, and how a matrix-vector tile, a softmax and a LayerNorm are laid
out in cycles on the `ref` profile. Every number and every listing below sits between `<!-- cookbook:NAME -->` markers and is regenerated by
`tests/programs/gen/cookbook.py` (`python3 tests/programs/gen/cookbook.py --update` rewrites the blocks; without `--update` it prints the difference).
`tests/programs/gen/test_cookbook.py` regenerates every block (the listings, the pager constraints, the geometry table and the division tables from the checked-in
measurement) on every run of `just kernel-cookbook` and fails when the document differs; `test_division.py` re-measures the division on the ISS and fails when that
measurement or the numbers below differ from it, so the annotations cannot drift from the kernels, the profile and the measurement. The listings are the program text the toolchain kernels emit (`opentsp-sw-toolchain/python/opentsp_tools/programs/kernels/`), the
programs of the `model/ts_*` cases; the division numbers are measured on the ISS.

## 1. Reading a listing

Each line is one instruction: its dispatch cycle, the cycles since the previous listed line (`+dt`), the queue, the instruction text and what it does. `E:n`
and `W:n` are stream `n` of row E or W; `.chain` takes the result that a previous VXM operation committed at the VXM station in the cycle before the read
(`assembly.md` §3). `d_func` and `d_skew` are the profile's latencies of the mnemonic (`config/ref.json`, `timing`). A producer dispatched at cycle `d`
commits its result `d_func` later; the consumer is dispatched so that the result, moved along its diagonal to the consumer's station, meets the consumer's
operand read, `d_skew` after the consumer's dispatch (the equation of `assembly.md`, "Scheduling a kernel against a profile"). A line of the form
`... n MEM reads (cycles a to b)` stands for MEM instructions the listing does not show; every instruction shown has an annotation, and a kernel that emits an
instruction the annotations do not know makes the generator fail.

## 2. Division and square root

The ISA has `RSqrt` and no reciprocal or divide instruction (the published TSP sources give RSqrt only). Three idioms form a reciprocal from `RSqrt`, plus a Newton
step. The domains below were **measured on the ISS** (`python3 tests/programs/gen/division_domains.py`): one vector of 320 positive f32 values per binade
(the two ends of the binade and random values inside it, 277 binades from 2^-149 to the largest f32), run through each idiom on the ISS and compared with the
exact quotient in float64, in ulps of the exact value; the numpy twin gave the same bits in every lane, and `tsp_sim` (the Sail model) the same words in every
program of the sweep and of the edge searches (`division_domains.py --sim`). The edges where the result stops being finite and
non-zero were then located to the last bit by searches that ran batches of candidates on the ISS. `tests/programs/vxm/division_edges.*` are golden programs
that pin the edge values and the special values on `tsp_sim` and the ISS; `test_division.py` repeats the measurement and fails when the numbers below or the
checked-in measurement (`tests/programs/gen/division_domains.json`) differ from it. The error figures are the largest over the sampled values, not a proof.

- **`rsqrt_sq`: `rsqrt(x * x)`.** Two chained ALUs. The result is `1/|x|`: the sign is lost, `x = 0` gives +inf for either zero, and `x = ±inf` gives 0.
  It is within 1 ulp wherever `x * x` is a normal f32. Where it is not, the result is wrong without a flag: `x * x` overflows to inf (the result is 0) from
  `x = 2^64`, and underflows to a subnormal (a growing error) or to 0 (the result is +inf) below 2^-63. The softmax kernel uses this idiom because its
  denominator, a sum that contains `exp(0) = 1`, lies in [1, n].
- **`rsqrt_pair`: `rsqrt(x) * rsqrt(x)`.** Two chained ALUs, the second reading one result as both operands. It is `1/x` for `x > 0` over the whole normal range
  and into the subnormals, at a price of up to 2 ulp (two roundings), and gives NaN for `x < 0` and +inf for either zero.
- **`newton_sq`, `newton_pair`: `y1 = y0 * (2 - x * y0)`.** Three chained ALUs, one Newton step on the seed `y0` of the first or the second idiom (the seed and
  `x` are read from MEM; the constant 2 is a vector). Without a fused multiply-add the product `x * y0` is rounded before the subtraction, so the step cannot
  make the seed better than about 1.5 ulp: from `rsqrt_sq` it does not lower the worst error (0.947 ulp before and ), from `rsqrt_pair` it lowers the worst error
  from 1.845 to 1.488 ulp over the whole domain. It needs `x > 0` (for `x < 0` and the seed of `rsqrt_sq` the product is -1 and the step returns 3 times the seed).

The three idioms as they are emitted (the VXM instructions of one group; the operands are read from MEM to meet them):

`rsqrt_sq`:

<!-- cookbook:listing-rsqrt-sq -->
```text
cycle  +dt  queue: instruction                                 ; meaning
  ... 4 MEM reads of the operands (cycle 2)
    9       VXM[0]: mul.f32 E:8, E:16, E:16                    ; x*x: both operands are the one stream; rounded to f32 (overflow gives inf, underflow a subnormal or 0); d_func=5 d_skew=2
   13   +4  VXM[1]: rsqrt.f32 E:12, E:8.chain                  ; rsqrt of the chained product, one cycle after it commits: 1/|x|; d_func=5
  ... 4 MEM writes of the result (cycle 21)
```
<!-- /cookbook:listing-rsqrt-sq -->

`rsqrt_pair`:

<!-- cookbook:listing-rsqrt-pair -->
```text
cycle  +dt  queue: instruction                                 ; meaning
  ... 4 MEM reads of the operands (cycle 2)
    9       VXM[0]: rsqrt.f32 E:8, E:16                        ; rsqrt(x): 1/sqrt(x), NaN for x < 0; d_func=5 d_skew=2
   13   +4  VXM[1]: mul.f32 E:12, E:8.chain, E:8.chain         ; the chained result twice, squared: 1/x for x > 0; d_func=5
  ... 4 MEM writes of the result (cycle 21)
```
<!-- /cookbook:listing-rsqrt-pair -->

One Newton step (`newton_sq` and `newton_pair` differ in the seed only):

<!-- cookbook:listing-newton -->
```text
cycle  +dt  queue: instruction                                 ; meaning
  ... 12 MEM reads of the operands (cycles 1 to 4)
    9       VXM[0]: mul.f32 E:8, E:16, E:20                    ; m = x*y0 (y0 the seed, a second operand stream); d_func=5 d_skew=2
  ... 4 MEM reads of the operands (cycle 9)
   13   +4  VXM[1]: sub.f32 E:12, E:24, E:8.chain              ; 2 - m: the constant 2 is read to meet it, m is chained; d_func=5
   17   +4  VXM[2]: mul.f32 E:8, E:28, E:12.chain              ; y1 = y0*(2 - m): the seed read again to meet it; d_func=5
  ... 4 MEM writes of the result (cycle 25)
```
<!-- /cookbook:listing-newton -->

Choosing: use `rsqrt_sq` when `x` is known to lie in its accurate domain (a softmax or a normalisation denominator); `rsqrt_pair` when `x` is positive and may be
very large or very small, if 2 ulp suffices; scale `x` into the domain first (a power of two is exact) when neither fits. A quotient `n / x` is `n * (1/x)`; its error
adds one rounding to the reciprocal's. No idiom here is correctly rounded: a kernel that needs the correctly rounded quotient has no instruction for it in 0.1.

## 3. A matrix-vector tile

`y = W x + b` for a 64 x 64 f16 matrix `W`, an f32 activation `x` and an f32 bias `b` (`opentsp_tools/programs/kernels/matvec_f16.py`, the program `model/ts_matvec_f16`). The matrix
is installed into MXM plane 0 (the weight reads, the LWs and an IW), `x` is narrowed to f16 on the VXM and broadcast (`abc`), the exact dot products are accumulated and
rounded once to f32 (`acc`), and the VXM adds the bias. The listing shows the weight reads as one line.

<!-- cookbook:listing-matvec -->
```text
cycle  +dt  queue: instruction                                 ; meaning
  ... 128 weight reads from the 16 slices of the matrix (the install) (cycles 6 to 16)
   24       MEM[4]: read xp0, E:0, 0x0                         ; read the f32 activation x (four byte planes) to meet the VXM narrowing
      ... 3 more, same meaning (same cycle)
   30   +6  MXM[0].LW: lw.f16 W:0                              ; load weights into the MXM buffer, one LW per cycle, from the weight words read earlier
      ... 1 more, same meaning (cycles 31)
   31   +0  VXM[0]: conv_f32_f16.f32 W:0, E:0                  ; narrow x to f16 on the VXM, result on row W towards the MXM; d_func=5 d_skew=2
   32   +1  MXM[0].LW: lw.f16 W:0                              ; load weights into the MXM buffer, one LW per cycle, from the weight words read earlier
      ... 5 more, same meaning (cycles 33, 34, 35, 36, 37)
   57  +20  MXM[0].LW: iw.f16                                  ; install the weights: IW makes the loaded matrix the active one
   58   +1  MXM[0].ABC: abc.f16 W:0                            ; activation broadcast and compute: the f16 vector against the 64 weight rows; d_func=20 d_skew=0
   78  +20  MXM[0].ACC: acc.f32 E:4, replace, 0                ; accumulate: the exact dot products rounded once to f32 onto row E, lane l = row l; d_func=20
  107  +29  MEM[8]: read bp0, E:8, 0x0                         ; read the f32 bias to meet the add
      ... 3 more, same meaning (same cycle)
  115   +8  VXM[1]: add.f32 E:12, E:4, E:8                     ; y = W x + b in f32 (the matrix result and the bias meet on the VXM); d_func=5 d_skew=2
  123   +8  MEM[44]: write 0x1080, E:12, 0x0                   ; store y (four byte planes) in the east quad
      ... 3 more, same meaning (same cycle)
```

## 4. Softmax

Softmax over each segment of four superlanes (64 lanes) of an f32 vector, as in `opentsp_tools/programs/kernels/softmax.py`: a per-superlane maximum, the two-round butterfly across the four
superlanes of a segment and a distribute (`reduce_bcast`), subtract, `exp`, the same for the sum, the reciprocal as `rsqrt(total * total)`, multiply. The SXM and MEM
traffic between the steps is summarised on `... ` lines; the dependency chain is the VXM and SXM lines.

<!-- cookbook:listing-softmax -->
```text

  ... 13 MEM reads and writes (cycles 15 to 61)

   81  +74  SXM.W.PRM: permute W:8, 0x1, E:4                   ; first butterfly stage: the superlane totals rotate by 2 superlanes (one byte plane per instruction); d_func=20

  ... 12 MEM reads and writes (cycles 115 to 119)
  125  +41  VXM[1]: max.f32 E:24, W:16, E:20                   ; combine: this round's sum or maximum with the rotated copy; d_func=5 d_skew=2
  ... 8 MEM reads and writes (cycles 133 to 137)
  161  +36  SXM.W.PRM: permute W:8, 0x2, E:4                   ; second stage: rotate by 1 superlane; d_func=20

  279   +4  VXM[4]: exp.f32 E:16, E:12.chain                   ; exp (chained): the exponentials, kept in MEM; d_func=5
```

## 5. LayerNorm

LayerNorm of a 64-lane f32 vector (`opentsp_tools/programs/kernels/layernorm.py`): the mean by `reduce_bcast`, the centred values (stored and read twice), the variance by a second
`reduce_bcast` of their squares, `rsqrt(var + eps)`, scale and shift, all chained on the VXM.

## 6. Long programs and patched programs

A queue's initial image holds `icu.iq_depth` / 4 units; a longer queue needs its text brought in by `ifetch` and `ifetchm` from SRAM that a package fills
(`assembly.md`, "Program-data packages"). The instruction pager turns a program whose queue images are too long into such a fetch-driven one. It is a toolchain
library, `opentsp_tools.pager` (`opentsp-sw-toolchain/python/opentsp_tools/`), together with the scheduling model it stands on (`profile`, `stations`, `sched`,
`queues`, `emit`) and the patch sites (`patch`). These are
the pager's constraints, as its module documents them:

<!-- cookbook:pager-constraints -->
```text
Constraints on the program. `page` refuses a program that breaks one with a ScheduleError naming the queue and the cycle;
tests/programs/gen/test_pager.py pins each refusal (the machine events behind them are in assembly.md, "Program-data
packages"):

  Barriers (Sync, Notify, Deskew, RuntimeDeskew). A queue behind a barrier no longer dispatches at the cycles of the view: a Sync
      parks it until the release cycle (a Notify at cycle N releases at N + barrier.release_latency), a Deskew or a RuntimeDeskew holds it
      to the next HAC epoch boundary (c2c.hac_period). The plan computes the occupancy of the IQ at every arrival from the cycles of the
      view, so after a barrier it is unknown: "after a barrier the cycles of the view are not dispatch cycles". `allow_waits=True` lifts the
      refusal for a caller that has already put the actual dispatch cycles into the view.
  Holds. A Repeat holds its queue through its last iteration, an AGT loop holds its path through its trailing countdowns, and a parcel
      that is due inside a hold waits. A Repeat on a queue that needs paging is refused (the plan does not model the head parcel's hold,
      "repeat on a paged queue"). Elsewhere the pager works around holds: a fetch is never placed in a cycle where the queue or the
      path it needs is held (`_windows`, `held`), and an `ifetchm` stays out of AGT loops.
  Control channel. The CU queue has no fetch and is never paged: a CU image longer than `icu.iq_depth` / 4 units is refused.
  Slices that host text. The words from `floor` (default: half of the slice) upwards of every MEM slice the pager uses are free
      (or the `regions` given); the pager takes its words from the top of the slice, outside every extern. A slice whose words
      are read onto a stream as text must run no SetVL or Config while it does (both gate or split the stream a Read puts out).
  Resources. A fetch needs a free stream (two consecutive cycles) and two free MEM slices of one quad for `ifetch`, an idle R path for
      `ifetchm`; where no cycle is legal the planner fails with the queue, the block and the cycles it tried.
```
<!-- /cookbook:pager-constraints -->

Patching. A bucket program is generated once and its operands that change between runs (a MEM address, a Permute map) are patch sites, located by assembling twice
(`opentsp_tools.patch.locate`). For a program that is not paged, `patch.asm_table` writes the located table in the format of `ots-asm patch` and
`patch.apply_object` patches an assembled object with it: the command writes the same bits as the host patcher (`patch.apply`), and re-checks every parcel it
touched (decode, slots, queue, bank rule), so a read moved into the bank of its write produces no object. `patch.apply` itself checks only ranges; the sites of a
paged program are in text blocks of a weight, outside the object the command patches.

## 7. Serving on other geometries

A serving generator does not name a profile and does not write the numbers of `ref` (320 lanes, a 256-lane tile, a chunk of 128 slots, five vocabulary rows per
word, 160 rows per install) into its text: it derives them from the profile (`opentsp_tools.programs.geometry`, `layout.Shape` in `opentsp_vllm.models.tinystories.layout`):

- **tile**: the largest power of two of superlanes up to 16, in lanes (256 on `ref`, 128 on 8 superlanes); an activation of more features is several tiles, and the
  butterfly of a reduction needs a power of two of superlanes. The superlanes above the tile stay idle on a wider chip.
- **chunk**: the slots of one score vector, at most the f16 rows one MXM plane installs (`mxm.rows / 2`) and the lanes of a vector, up to 128 (128 on `ref`, 64 on 8
  superlanes). A K block, a V block and the context of a bucket follow it: on 8 superlanes only the contexts of 64 slots exist.
- **pack**: the vocabulary rows of one embedding or head word, five segments of 64 lanes where the vector has 320 lanes or more, `lanes / 64` below; the head installs
  the profile's f16 rows per plane (`mxm.rows / 2` up to 160) per install, and the logit rows are `pack * installs` words.
- **unit lanes**: the lanes of the unit that sums an MXM dot product are the profile's `vlanes`; they decide the sign of an exact zero sum, so the host twin takes them
  as an argument (`paged_reference.step_batch(..., unit=vlanes, pack=pack)`).
- **install group**: an LW reads 16 streams from 16 slices. A hemisphere with 16 or more slices supplies them (`ref`, `test/sl8`, `test/sl32`); with 8 (`test/ref_small`)
  the plane's 8 and the first 8 of the other hemisphere do (every MEM slice writes both rows, so either hemisphere reaches either matrix input), and the install starts
  early enough for the farther slices. Kernels that name quads fold the caller's quad number onto the quads the profile has (the same slices, other words).

A profile that cannot hold a program is refused before any scheduling, with the first requirement it does not meet and both numbers: `needs.SERVE` (superlanes,
MXM rows, MEM slices per hemisphere) and `needs.model_needs` for the serving step, the planner for the words a region needs in a bank, `geometry.check_install`
for a matrix that does not fit a plane. A profile larger than a program needs fits; the programs that remain written for exactly the geometry of `ref` (the one-token
step, the block, the fixed ) say "written for exactly" with both numbers. The table is generated from the code; `tests/showcase/tinystories/test_serve_geometry.py`
runs the batched step on `test/sl32` (the test model of `ref`) and on `test/sl8` (a small model that its plane holds) on the ISS against the host reference, bit for bit.

The serving binding of vLLM is written for the geometry of `ref` (its host side packs five rows per word, reads 63 head installs of 160 rows and 320-byte output words):
its `profile` option accepts the profiles of that geometry and refuses the others with the first number that differs. On `test/ref_small` the kernel cases of the
serving programs run (`model/ts_matvec_f16`, `ts_scores`, `ts_layernorm`, `ts_softmax128`, `ts_trees`, `ts_write_row`); the batched step does not, because its floor plan
names eleven quads per hemisphere and that profile has two.
