# TSP reference architecture: chip topology and geometry

The profile defines the geometry used by the Sail model. Queue numbers and station assignments below are the OpenTSP interface. The [architecture guide](../README.md) identifies the executable contract and the [timing rules](microarchitecture.md) define when each station reads and writes.

**Profile parameters.** Profiles configure superlane count, SRAM size/address width, MEM slices, matrix structure, extension gates and station gaps. The rest of the geometry (45 stream registers, 32 + 32 streams, 88 MEM queue IDs and 144 queues) is fixed. Ten layout keys move stations within the nine ordering relations of config/schema.json; each unit remains on its specified side of the next.

**Timing does not follow geometry.** Changing a station gap, quad count or matrix row count does not derive new timing rows. The causal coupling is d_func − d_skew ≥ geometry.superlanes for cross-superlane rows (§3). The profile-derivation tool offers starting defaults, not circuit timing measurements.

- `geometry.superlanes`: any count from 1 to 32. `mini` has 1, `test/sl8` has 8, `test/mid_min` and `test/fetch_alt` have 4, `ref` has 20 and `test/sl32` has 32. 32 is the largest count that two layouts hold: the 5-bit superlane field of an error-log record (`microarchitecture.md` §16) and the 9-bit lane count of Shift (512 lanes). A count above 32 changes those layouts and is not part of this version. Every row of the timing table that depends on the count stays an implementation choice: nothing here is a silicon figure, and a profile of more than 20 superlanes needs `d_func − d_skew ≥ geometry.superlanes` on the cross-superlane rows (§3).
- `sram.addr_bits`: 13 to 16, the width of an SRAM address (13 on `ref` and `mini`). A slice has at most 2^`addr_bits` words (schema relation `sram.bank_bit` < `sram.addr_bits`).
- `sram.words_per_slice`: any power of two from 256 to 65536 and at most 2^`sram.addr_bits`, with `sram.bank_bit` = log₂(`words_per_slice`) − 1 (schema relation; see "Banking" in §6).
- `geometry.mem_slices_per_hemisphere`: a multiple of 4 from 4 to 44 (the number of quads is this value / 4). MEM slice s exists when (s mod 44) < the key; a hemisphere's absent slices are its outermost ones. The queue map and the global slice ids stay those of `ref`, so a program's queue names mean the same thing on every profile. An absent slice has no station and its queue accepts nothing (OTS-E-QUE-002; `microarchitecture.md` §3). The layout relations of `config/schema.json` count quads instead of 11.
- `mxm.rows`: a multiple of 16 from 16 to 320: the stream values a weight buffer or an installed matrix holds, and the number of lanes an ABC produces (`microarchitecture.md` §6).
- `mxm.planes_per_hemisphere`: 1 or 2 (2 on `ref` and `mini`). The queue map is fixed (3 queues per plane, `design-space.md`), so the plane count above 2 needs a larger queue map and is not part of this version; with 1 the second plane of each hemisphere and its three queues do not exist (OTS-E-QUE-002), the planes are 0 (west) and 1 (east), and no plane can hold f16 (an f16 pair needs both planes of a hemisphere). Profile `test/one_plane`.
- `mxm.acc_depth`: 2 to 32 accumulators per plane (2 on `ref` and `mini`): the `ACC` index below it decodes, bit 12 as its low bit and bits 11 to 8 above. Profile `test/acc8`.
- `mxm.f16_rows`: from `mxm.rows`/2 to `mxm.rows`, a multiple of 8 (`mxm.rows`/2 on `ref`, 160): the rows of an f16 matrix, so the capacity of an f16 weight buffer and installed matrix is 2 × `mxm.f16_rows` stream values (the 160 × 320 of HC34 p.28 or the 320 × 320 of ISCA20 §III-D). Capacity only; the f16 rate is `timing.mxm_abc.occupancy_f16`. The relation to `mxm.rows` is checked by every harness (`timing_config_error`), not by the schema. Profile `test/f16_tandem`.
- `mxm.fp8_rate`: 1 or 2 (1 on `ref` and `mini`): the FP8 ABCs a plane accepts in one cycle, relative to INT8 (`microarchitecture.md` §6). Profile `test/fp8_rate2`; `test/mxm_wide` uses every extreme of the four keys on a 16-row matrix.
- `mxm.acc_bits`: 32 or 128 (32 on `ref` and `mini`), and 128 exactly when `extensions` lists `wide_acc`: the width of an accumulator lane (the ACC type `x128`).
- `extensions`: a required list of the optional extensions the profile declares, from `bf16`, `wide_acc` and `block_fp8`; `[]` on `ref` and `mini`. A word that uses an extension that is not listed does not decode (OTS-E-DEC-003).
- `icu.iq_depth`: instruction queue capacity, 1280 bytes in `ref` and `mini`. Each queue stores 32-bit text units. A full queue refuses an excess unit as described in [the instruction front-end contract](microarchitecture.md#3-instruction-front-end-barrier-and-icu-slicescucu_iqsail-postludeiqsail-postludeticksail-slicescucu_instssail).
- `validation.md` §3.1 lists which keys keep conformance.

## 2. Floorplan

The chip is split into a **west** and an **east hemisphere**, with the VXM at the bisection (HC34 p.14). From the outer edge inward, each hemisphere contains: MXM, SXM, 44 MEM slices, then the VXM in the centre (ISCA20 Fig. 4, §II-B; HC34 p.28 to p.30, whose banners read `MXM | SXM | MEM | VXM | MEM | SXM | MXM`). **HC34 contradicts itself here:** the banner of HC34 p.31 reads `MEM | MXM | SXM | VXM | SXM | MXM | MEM`, with MEM outermost. The specification follows p.28 to p.30 and ISCA20 §II-B ("44 slices numbered 0 to 43 with slice MEM0 closest to the VXM and MEM43 nearest to the SXM"), which agree with each other, and reads the p.31 banner as a slide-authoring slip; the SXM box drawn on p.31 is unaffected and stays the source of the SXM stations (§4). Both banners were read from the rendered slides (p.30 and p.31 as images, §5).

- **MEM:** 44 slices per hemisphere on `ref` (`geometry.mem_slices_per_hemisphere` slices on a smaller profile, §1), numbered 0..43, with MEM 0 nearest the VXM and MEM 43 nearest the SXM (ISCA20 §II-B). Global slice ids: west 0..43, east 44..87. Slices are grouped in **quads** (q = (slice mod 44) / 4, 0..10 on `ref`) that share a stream-register station: HC34 p.30 "quad timing groups".
- **MXM:** 4 planes on `ref` and `mini` (2 × `mxm.planes_per_hemisphere`, 2 or 4) of `mxm.rows` × `vlanes` MACCs (320 × 320 on `ref`). Planes 0 and 1 are west, 2 and 3 east (ISCA20 §III-D; HC34 p.28).
- **SXM:** one per hemisphere (ISCA20 §III-E). Each has 4 distributors, 2 transposers, 2 lane shifters, a permuter and a select (HC34 p.31; ISCA20 Fig. 8).
- **VXM:** one, at the centre, with 16 ALUs per lane in a 4 × 4 mesh (ISCA20 §III-C; HC34 p.14).
- **C2C:** `c2c.links` = 16 links per chip on `ref` and `mini` (ISCA20 §II: "a total of sixteen (16) ×4 links"), at most 128 in a profile; each Send, Receive and Transmit names its link. The C2C queues are 2 TX and 2 RX per hemisphere, counted in the 14-queue SXM blocks (§5). ISCA22's partition into 7 local and 4 global links is a recorded provenance conflict (`microarchitecture.md` §10).
- **ICU:** 144 instruction queues plus a control channel (§5).

## 3. Lanes, superlanes and the stagger

- A vector has VL = 16 × superlanes lanes of one byte each. A **superlane** is 16 lanes, the cross-section of all slices handled by one tile (ISCA20 §II).
- A 16-byte memory word is one superlane: byte l of a word is lane l (little-endian; ISCA20 §II-B).
- Multi-byte elements are byte planes in width-aligned consecutive streams: a 16-bit element uses streams s, s+1 with s even; a 32-bit element uses s..s+3 with s ≡ 0 mod 4. Byte b of the element is in stream s+b (ISCA20 §III-C SG4).
- **Stagger.** The timing tables use superlane 0 as the reference. Superlane k receives the same operation k cycles later. Dispatch, operand read and result commit have separate times. On a same-superlane path, data and instructions share the stagger. A cross-superlane consumer may need a value from the last superlane for an output on the first. For Shift, Permute, ABC and ABC8, every effective timing row must therefore satisfy `d_func − d_skew ≥ geometry.superlanes`. Map readiness has the same minimum dissemination interval. For example, on ref an override with skew 15 needs a functional latency of at least 35 even though the chip has only 20 superlanes. See [timing and override validation](microarchitecture.md).
- **The bound is causal, not physical.** `d_func − d_skew ≥ geometry.superlanes` is the least interval in which the last superlane's input can have arrived (superlane k runs k cycles later); it counts no transport inside the chip. The shipped Shift, Permute, ABC and ABC8 rows sit exactly on it (`ref`: 20 + 0 against 20 superlanes). An implementation that cannot move a lane across `superlanes − 1 − k` tiles in `1 + k` cycles must declare a larger `d_func` for those rows in its own profile; the model's check admits it and no row of the shipped profiles claims that the minimum is physically reachable.

## 4. Stream rows and stations

- There are two **rows**: **E** (flows eastward) and **W** (flows westward). Each has 32 streams (ids 0..31) and 45 stream registers, numbered 0..44 in flow order (ISCA20 §II, Fig. 4). A stream reference is encoded as 6 bits: bit 5 is the row (0 = E, 1 = W) and bits 4..0 the stream id. Assembly: `E:n`, `W:n`.
- A unit at **gap g** reads register g and writes register g+1. A value written at gap g in cycle c is visible to a reader at gap G > g exactly in cycle c + (G − g): one register per cycle (HC34 p.24). It falls off after register 44 and is replaced only by a producer upstream of the reader.
- **Station gaps** per row, in flow order (the same on both rows, mirrored; ISCA20 Fig. 4; HC34 p.31). Which row each unit reads and writes is given per unit in `encoding.md`, "Stations" (`encoding.json` `stations`, checked against the model on every queue):

| Gap | Unit | Reads | Writes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | MXM ACC output | — | the row flowing toward the VXM from that MXM |
| 4 | SXM transposer and select output (ISCA20 Fig. 4, HC34 p.31); shifter and permuter output (modeling choice below); see the note on the OR below | — | the other row |
| 5 | C2C RX | — | the other row |
| 18 − q | MEM quad q of the upstream hemisphere | yes | yes |
| 21 | VXM | yes | yes (both rows) |
| 24 + q | MEM quad q of the downstream hemisphere | yes | yes |
| 38 | C2C TX; C2C RX for Ifetch only (an RX unit reads no operand stream, and fetches its instruction text where the TX units of its hemisphere read) | yes | — |
| 39 | SXM distributor | yes | yes (same row) |
| 40 | SXM transposer, permuter and select input (HC34 p.31); shifter input (modeling choice below) | yes | — |
| 43 | MXM LW / IW / ABC input | yes | — |

**Verified against the slide as drawn.** The gaps 38, 39 and 40 rest on a graphic, not on prose, so they were checked on the rendered HC34 p.31 (the SXM box, read as an image). The lower row reads, left to right, `43 | 43 | 41 | SEL | PERM | TSP | 40 | DST | 39 | TX | 38`, and the upper row `3 | 4 | [OR] | 5 | RX`. Under the rule above (a unit at gap g reads register g and writes register g + 1; a unit drawn between registers g and g + 1 sits at gap g) TX is at 38, DST at 39 and SEL, PERM and TSP at 40, exactly as in the table; exactly two arrows enter the OR, from SEL and from TSP, and its output arrow ends at register 5; PERM has no outgoing arrow and no shifter is drawn; one long arrow runs from register 39 leftwards to register 3 on the upper row (§7). The two leftmost boxes of the lower row both read 43; the slide most likely means 42 and 43, and the model reads no register 42 either way.

**The OR at register 5.** HC34 p.31 draws the SEL and TSP outputs of the SXM going into a box labelled OR whose output enters register 5 of the other row; the PERM and DST outputs do not feed it. ISCA20 Fig. 4 draws the SXM outputs joining the row after register 4. The model follows the figure: when the select (SEL) and a transposer (TSP) of one hemisphere both write the same stream at the turn-out in the same commit cycle, the register receives the bitwise OR of the two values and no OTS-E-SRF-002 is reported for that pair (`commit_due` in `postlude/tick.sail`; `microarchitecture.md` §2). Every other same-cycle pair or group is still a collision: a shifter or the permuter with a SEL or TSP write, two TSP writes, or three or more writers. The sources do not say what the OR does beyond its label, so the extent of the merge (exactly one SEL write and one TSP write of one hemisphere) is an opentsp decision (`isa-db/provenance.yaml` `abstractions.sxm_or_merge`). **Gap 4 versus register 5:** the model writes the turn-out at gap 4, and a unit at gap g writes register g+1 (above), so the OR output is in register 5 as drawn; the figures agree with the model's gap.

**Shifter and permuter stations.** The gaps 40 and 4 are drawn for the transposer and the select: ISCA20 Fig. 4 and HC34 p.31 show their outputs crossing to the other row. HC34 p.31 also draws the permuter's input at 40. Neither figure draws an output for the permuter, and neither draws a shifter at all. opentsp places the shifter at read gap 40 and write gap 4 and the permuter's output at gap 4, the stations of the other turn-round units, so that all of them have one timing (`isa-db/provenance.yaml` `abstractions.sxm_shf_prm_stations`).

The upstream hemisphere for the E row is the west hemisphere, and for the W row the east hemisphere. `core/layout.sail` (`read_gap`, `write_gap`) gives every unit's gaps. A unit with no station on a row cannot read or write it (OTS-E-DIR-001). A MEM slice s with (s mod 44) ≥ `geometry.mem_slices_per_hemisphere` has no station at all; its queue is OTS-E-QUE-002 (§1, §5). Quad q keeps its gap (18 − q or 24 + q) on every profile, so a smaller profile only uses fewer gaps (8..18 and 24..34 on `ref`); the schema relations that keep the C2C and MXM stations out of the MEM range count the quads of the profile (`mem_slices_per_hemisphere` / 4, instead of 11) and are written as an `offset` plus `quads` × that count in `config/schema.json`, `x-opentsp-relations`: an `offset` plus `quads` × (`mem_slices_per_hemisphere` / 4)).

## 5. Instruction queues

There are 144 independent queues (ISCA20 §II), plus the control channel `0xFFFF`. One parcel dispatches per queue per cycle; a parcel is one instruction, except that a MEM slice queue's parcel holds one instruction per slot: one fetch (an IfetchM also takes the read port), one read and one write per liquid thread, and one AGT configuration instruction (`microarchitecture.md` §3; encoding.json `mem_slots`). The sources conflict here, and the model follows HC34 p.23 ("One instruction issued per cycle at each dispatch path") over ISCA20 §II ("each can issue one or more instructions per cycle"); this is a decision. ISCA20 establishes the total of 144 data queues. OpenTSP assigns them to the following blocks and fixes the role and number of each queue so that a compiler can place instructions unambiguously. In particular, the two transmit and two receive queues per hemisphere occupy slots inside the 14-queue reshape/link block. HC34 p.31 locates link stations on the floorplan. Station placement alone does not determine queue numbering.

| Queues | Unit | Count |
|---|---|---|
| 0..43 | MEM west slices 0..43 | 44 |
| 44..87 | MEM east slices 0..43 | 44 |
| 88..103 | VXM ALUs 0..15 | 16 |
| 104..109 | MXM west: plane 0 (104..106), plane 1 (107..109) | 6 |
| 110..115 | MXM east: plane 2 (110..112), plane 3 (113..115) | 6 |
| 116..129 | SXM west: DST 116..119, TSP 120..121, SHF 122..123, PRM 124, SEL 125, C2C TX 126..127, C2C RX 128..129 | 14 |
| 130..143 | SXM east (same order): DST 130..133, TSP 134..135, SHF 136..137, PRM 138, SEL 139, C2C TX 140..141, C2C RX 142..143 | 14 |
| 0xFFFF | control channel (NOP, Sync, Notify, Config only) | — |

The ICU instructions (NOP, Sync, Notify, Repeat, Ifetch, Config, SetVL) issue on every data queue (HC34 p.27); the control channel takes NOP, Sync, Notify and Config only (`microarchitecture.md` §3). Deskew and RuntimeDeskew (C2C slice) also issue on every data queue (`microarchitecture.md` §10). All other instructions issue only on the queues of their unit (OTS-E-QUE-002).

## 6. Memory

- 88 slices × 8192 words × 16 bytes per superlane = 220 MiB at 20 superlanes (ISCA20 §II-B; HC34 p.30). That is `ref`; with a 14-bit address (16384 words, `test/w14`) 440 MiB, 15 bits 880 MiB, 16 bits 1760 MiB (computed: 88 × words × 320 bytes); a profile has 2 × `mem_slices_per_hemisphere` slices of `words_per_slice` words (§1; the slice and queue numbering does not change, the absent slices simply do not exist). The harness `S` and `D` lines of an absent slice are input errors (`porting-guide.md` §3).
- Each slice has **2 banks** selected by the bank bit (ISCA20 §II-B Eq. 2 "2 banks/slice"; HC34 p.30 "from 2 banks"; the ATPESC 2022 figure label "4 banks of memory" is a recorded conflict, `isa-db/provenance.yaml` `conflicts: sram.banks`). OpenTSP uses the most significant implemented address bit to choose between two contiguous banks of equal size. This is bit 12 on ref and bit 7 on mini (bit 13, 14, 15 on the 14, 15, 16-bit test profiles). A direct Read and Write to tiles of the same thread can share a cycle only when the addresses select different banks. Changing the selector would change program legality. It is pseudo-dual-ported: at most one read and one write per cycle, in different banks (ISCA20 §III-B).
- **Banking.** `sram.bank_bit` names the address bit that selects the bank: bit `log₂(words_per_slice) − 1`, so two contiguous banks of equal size (a schema relation that Sail cannot state, added by `tools/schema_invariants.py`; bit 12 on `ref`, 7 on `mini`). The key is not free because every consumer of the rule assumes two banks: `sram_bank` in `core/sram.sail`, the bank tests of `postlude/tick.sail` (MEM-001, the IfetchM reservation), the ISS memory slice, the harness bank test, the Python oracle and the directed tests of MEM-001 (`validation.md` directed tests and golden vectors pin the rule). Two banks follow the published sources (ISCA20 Eq. 2 and HC34 p.30); the label "4 banks" of one later talk is a recorded conflict (`isa-db/provenance.yaml` `conflicts: sram.banks`). **What N banks or a low-order interleave would need:** a function `sram_bank(addr)` with a range of N values in place of one bit, keys for the bank count and the select (most or least significant bits), the same-bank rule generalised from "different bit" to "different bank", and every test above regenerated for the new keys. That general bank-select function is not part of this interface. A program's legality (which Read and Write may share a cycle) depends on the bank rule, so a profile with another rule is another interface, with another profile digest.
- Bounds: each slice has a segment size (`SetSegment`, reset to the whole slice). A reference at or above it, or below 0 after an AGT step, is OTS-E-OPR-001; addresses do not wrap.
- Each slice queue may dispatch, per liquid thread, one read-type and one write-type reference per cycle, in different banks; references of different threads never clash (`microarchitecture.md` §3, §4).

## 7. What is not modelled

The following are not modelled; each is listed in `microarchitecture.md` §9 with its source:
The data-path ECC, instruction-text ECC and error CSR log are modelled (`microarchitecture.md` §§15–16). The remaining omissions are:
- physical power modes (SetVL output/storage masking and MEM Config liquid threads are modelled, with input integrity checks retained under their owning rules; physical power gating and power transitions are not);
- the redundant superlane and its restagger flops.

The following two published features are not modelled (or only partly), and their sources are given here:
- **The ISCA22 Dragonfly topology between nodes.** The model has all `c2c.links` links (`microarchitecture.md` §10). The harness wires them as follows (`tsp_sim --chips K`, `c_emulator/tsp_sim.c`; the same generator is `tools/gen_topology.py dragonfly K`). With K = 1, every link loops back. With K = 2..8 there is one node: the fully connected node of ISCA22 §2.2 over local links 0..6. With K above 8 there are N = ⌈K/8⌉ nodes of 8 chips (the last holds the rest), each wired as above over its own chips, and global links 7..10 join the nodes all-to-all, with one global link between every pair of nodes (the group level of the Dragonfly, ISCA22 §2.1, §2.2); a global link whose far chip does not exist (the last node is not full) is not connected. Links that are not connected (links l ≥ K − 1 in a node of K < 8 chips, global links with no peer, links above 10) behave as unconnected links (OTS-E-OPR-001). Every transfer on a link has a packet type (data, csr, sync, ctrl; `microarchitecture.md` §10), and the control unit of a chip sends csr packets (a remote CSR access, §14) over one link of this wiring to the chip at its far end; there is no routing, and every chip has its own host interface. K above 264 (33 nodes: every node pair then still has a global port) is rejected (exit 2); a larger system needs a topology file (below).
- **Modeled system scale and addressing limits.** ISCA22 §2.2 publishes: "the TSP system can scale out up to 33 nodes for total of 33×8 = 264 TSPs by providing full connectivity between all of the nodes", and, with racks as the local group, "145 (racks)×72 (TSPs per rack) for a total of 10,440 TSPs in the largest system". ISCA22 §4.4 also describes "The local group radix-8 torus topology" and says "we triple-connect physical links within the torus to increase the nearest-neighbor throughput". The specification's limits are as follows. (1) The `chip_id` of Send, Receive, Transmit and DeskewRecv is 9 bits: bits 21..16 carry the low 6 bits, and bits 2..0 carry the high 3. A program can name up to 512 chips. (2) The `link` field is 7 bits: bits 9..6 carry the low 4 bits, and bits 5..3 carry the high 3. `c2c.links` is at most 128. (3) The harness wires `--chips K` for K = 1..264 (above), and any other system from a topology file (next paragraph). (4) The radix-8 torus, a tray and the triple-connected links are generators of the topology file, not a model feature. A link is architecturally only an in-order FIFO with a latency (`microarchitecture.md` §10), so a larger or differently shaped system adds no instruction semantics. It changes only the wiring, which belongs to the harness and is not architectural state; routing stays software (a relay program on an intermediate chip, `microarchitecture.md` §10).
- **The topology file** (`tsp_sim --topology topo.json`, `tsp-iss --topology topo.json`, `tsp-iss run --topology topo.json`, `ots-asm mpack --topology topo.json`; it gives the number of chips and replaces `--chips`). A JSON object with `chips` (1..512), `links` (a list of `{"a": [chip, link], "b": [chip, link], "latency": N}`: each entry joins two link ends, in both directions, and names every end once; the same end twice loops a link back on itself; `latency` is optional and applies to both directions), `latency` (the default latency of the links that name none), `jitter_seed` (the same as `--link-jitter-seed`, used when no link option is given) and `cycles_per_vector` (a check: it must be the profile's `c2c.cycles_per_vector`, since the link rate is a profile key and not wiring). Every latency is in [`c2c.link_latency_min`, `c2c.link_latency_max`]; a link that no entry names is not connected. `--link-latency N` and `--link-latency-seed S` override every latency of the file. An unknown key, an end outside the chips or the profile's links, an end named twice, a repeated key, text after the object and an error in a value are input errors (exit 2) with the same message from `tsp_sim` and `tsp-iss` (`topology FILE:...`). Generators (`opentsp-arch-spec/tools/gen_topology.py`; the shapes are opentsp choices, the published sources give the node and the group level only): `node8` (one node of 8 chips, the same wiring as `--chips 8`), `dragonfly K` (the wiring of `--chips K`), `torus R` (R × R chips in a two-dimensional torus, each pair of neighbours joined by three parallel links as ISCA22 §4.4 describes, link 3d + t for direction d = east, west, north, south and copy t = 0..2) and `tray8` (a tray: 8 chips in a ring, each neighbour pair joined by two parallel links).
### Relay through an intermediate chip

Two chips that no link joins (a larger wiring, such as the 64-chip system, does not join every pair) talk through a third chip: routing is a program on the intermediate chip B, not a hardware function. A link is an in-order FIFO, so vectors arrive in the order they were sent.

1. **A.** A west slice reads the word onto a stream, and `send {chip_id}, {link}, {stream}` puts it on the link toward B at cycle T.
2. **B.** The vector arrives on the receive queue of the link at T + L (L = `c2c.link_latency`). `receive {chip_id}, {link}, {stream}` writes it to a stream at the receive gap (`layout.c2c_rx_gap`); it reaches the transmit gap (`layout.c2c_tx_gap`) `c2c_tx_gap - c2c_rx_gap` cycles later, where a `send` on the link toward C forwards it. No MEM slice is needed: the vector stays in the stream registers.
3. **C.** `receive` at the arrival, then a MEM write stores it.

**Cost.** A hop costs L. The relay adds `d_func(c2c) + (c2c_tx_gap - c2c_rx_gap)` cycles at B, which is 1 + 33 = 34 cycles on `ref`, so the end-to-end latency from A's `send` to C's `receive` is 2 L + 34 cycles (L = 216: 466 cycles on `ref`). A vector occupies each link it crosses for `c2c.cycles_per_vector` cycles: a second `send` on a link fewer cycles after the first sends nothing (LNK-001; LNK-003 for a link that a control packet occupies). `test/c2c/relay_directed.py` (run by `model_test.sh`) runs this relay on the simulator and checks these figures.

**The DeskewRecv form.** The schedule above fixes the link latency L into the program: B and C receive at T + L, so the program is right for one link latency only. The form that does not depend on L replaces `receive {chip_id}, {link}, {stream}` at B and at C with `deskew_recv {chip_id}, {link}, {stream}`: the queue is held to the next HAC epoch boundary after its dispatch and dispatches as a `receive` at the first boundary at which a vector has arrived on the link (`isa.md`; `microarchitecture.md` section 10, "Deterministic synchronisation"). The receive time is then a boundary, not T + L, for every latency below `c2c.hac_period`. The cycle cost of this form is not measured: the figures above are those of the fixed-latency form that `relay_directed.py` checks.

## Changing the reference architecture

A configuration can vary only the keys and limits defined above. Changing an instruction layout, fixed queue map or architectural behavior requires updating the model, catalogue, generated encoding, tools, tests and conformance vectors together. See [Extending](extending.md).
